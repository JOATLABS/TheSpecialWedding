Planner Interaction Flows

Rail Targeting Logic

What determines which vendors show in the rail?

Now (Phase 1): Location-based. The rail shows sponsored vendors whose service area matches the planner's location. The vendor's location is displayed on each card.

Later (AI-enhanced): The AI recommendation engine uses additional signals — the planner's existing weddings, vendors already in their directory, booking history, category gaps, budget tiers, wedding timeline — to rank and filter which sponsored vendors appear. But the foundation is always location.

Where the rail appears: Homepage (/planner), Vendor page (/vendors), Budget page (/budget). Same component, same data. Vertical on desktop (lg+), horizontal scroll on mobile.

The rail is NOT wedding-specific. It shows vendors in the planner's area. The wedding context only enters the picture when the planner takes an action (Request Quote opens a modal where they pick the wedding).

Flow 1: "Add to Directory"

Trigger: Planner clicks "Add to Directory" on a sponsored vendor card in the rail

Steps:
1. Vendor info (name, category, email, service area, image) is copied into the planner's personal vendor_profiles collection as a new entry
2. New VendorProfile is tagged with source: "sponsored_ad" and campaign_id for attribution
3. Button changes to "In Directory" (green, disabled) — cannot add twice
4. An ad_lead record is created with lead_type: "add_to_directory"
5. An ad_event is recorded with event_type: "vendor_added_to_directory"

What the planner does next: Goes to their Vendors page, sees the new vendor in their directory, and can assign them to any wedding from there using the existing vendor assignment flow.

Why not "Add to Wedding" directly? The planner may manage multiple weddings. The vendor goes into their directory (a shared resource across all weddings) first. Assignment to a specific wedding is a separate, intentional decision.

Flow 2: "Request Quote"

Trigger: Planner clicks "Request Quote" on a sponsored vendor card

Steps:
1. A centered modal popup opens (does NOT navigate away, no layout shift)
2. Planner selects which wedding this quote is for (dropdown of their active weddings)
3. AI pre-drafts a quote request email using: selected wedding context (date, location, guest count, budget), vendor category, and planner's name/business
4. Planner can edit the AI draft freely, or delete it and write their own message
5. Planner clicks "Send Quote Request"
6. Email is sent to the vendor's registered email address
7. Vendor is auto-added to planner's directory if not already there (tagged source: "sponsored_ad")
8. An ad_lead is created with lead_type: "quote_request", the message content, click_id, and wedding_id
9. An ad_event is recorded with event_type: "lead_created"
10. The lead appears in the vendor's portal inbox (/portal/ads/leads)
11. Vendor receives a notification to respond

Why a modal? Eliminates layout shift issues. The planner stays exactly where they were. Clicking outside or pressing Escape closes it.

AI draft is a suggestion, not mandatory. The planner always has full control over what gets sent. The AI just saves them time by starting with context they'd otherwise have to type.

Why wedding selector in the modal? The rail is location-based (not wedding-specific). When the planner wants to request a quote, THAT is when they specify which wedding it's for. This is also when the AI uses that wedding's data to draft the email.

Flow 3: "Hide Vendor" (via card dismiss)

Trigger: Planner clicks X / dismiss on a sponsored card, or uses the drawer's hide option

Steps:
1. Vendor ID added to planner's planner_ad_preferences.hidden_vendor_ids
2. Card disappears from rail immediately
3. ad_event recorded with event_type: "dismiss"
4. Vendor never shown to this planner again (across all weddings)
5. If 3+ unique planners hide the same campaign in 7 days, campaign auto-paused for root admin review

Resolved Design Gaps (23 items)

VENDOR PORTAL

V1. Vendor Signup/Onboarding Flow

Decision: Vendor arrives at vendor.thespecialwedding.io signup page. After submitting basic info (name, email, category, location), they see a "Thank you, your account is pending approval" screen. No email verification yet.

Root admin gets in-app notification (bell icon) of the new signup. Admin reviews and approves. On approval, an email with a temporary password is sent to the vendor. Vendor signs in, changes password, completes their profile.

Free-tier shortcut: Root admin has a "Create Vendor" button on their advertising dashboard that links directly to the vendor campaign creation page. Admin can also click on any vendor card in the vendor list to "Log in as vendor" and see/operate their portal as they do.

Profile completeness gate: Vendor must complete: business_name, email, service_category, location, and at least one image before they can create a campaign.

V2. Vendor Profile / Settings

Decision: Settings modal accessible from the vendor portal sidebar. Contains: business name, email, phone, website URL, service category, service area/location, logo/image, business description. This is the info that shows on planner-facing sponsored cards.

Profile vs creative split: Profile fields (name, email, location, category) update live across all campaigns. Creative fields (headline, description, offer, CTA, ad image) are frozen per campaign and require re-review if changed on a live campaign.

V3. Campaign Resubmission After Rejection

Decision: In-app notification banner (same bell/banner pattern as planner and root admin dashboards). When admin requests changes, vendor sees a banner on their campaign page: "Changes requested: [admin notes]". Campaign returns to draft status so vendor can edit and resubmit.

V4. Creative Preview in Context

Decision: Show a live preview of the sponsored card in the right side panel of the campaign creation form. As the vendor fills in headline, description, offer, CTA, and uploads an image, the preview updates in real-time showing exactly how their ad will look in the planner's rail.

Pros: Vendor sees result as they type. Reduces back-and-forth with admin. Sets quality expectations.
Cons: Requires responsive layout on the wizard page (form left, preview right). On mobile, preview collapses below the form. Worth it.

V5. Vendor Notification System

Decision: In-portal/app notifications using the existing notification module. Bell icon in vendor portal header with unread count badge. Notifications for: campaign approved, campaign rejected, changes requested, new lead from planner, campaign ended/completed. Email also sent for critical events (approval, rejection, new lead) using existing email service (Resend).

V6. Vendor Responds to Quote Request

Decision: Vendor replies via their own email provider. The quote request email they receive has a Reply-To set to the planner's email. No in-portal reply UI needed. Lead status transitions: when vendor sends a reply email, they manually mark it as "Responded" in their leads page (or it stays pending until they do). Keeps the system simple and avoids building a full messaging layer.

PLANNER

P7. Empty Rail State

Decision: Rail collapses/hides entirely when there are no matching campaigns. No "No vendors in your area" message, no empty box. The main content area expands to fill the full width. Rail only appears when there are 1+ active campaigns matching the planner's location. This also means the grid layout dynamically adjusts: lg:grid-cols-4 (with rail) vs lg:grid-cols-1 (without rail).

P8. "Why am I seeing this?" (FTC Compliance)

Decision: Needs further FTC research before implementation. Current: small text tag on each card showing targeting rationale ("Based on your location"). Future: clickable tooltip/popover with full disclosure per FTC native advertising guidelines. Marked as a research item to be resolved before public launch.

P9. Planner Has No Active Weddings

Decision: Remove the wedding selector dropdown from the quote modal. Planner can still request a quote or add vendor to directory without having a wedding. Quote email is drafted without wedding-specific context (just planner name + general inquiry). When planner later creates a wedding, the vendor is already in their directory ready to be assigned. The wedding_id on the ad_lead is optional (null when no wedding selected).

P10. "Show Fewer Sponsored Vendors" Toggle

Decision: "Show fewer" link at the bottom of the rail. Clicking it sets planner_ad_preferences.reduced_frequency = true. When enabled, the rail shows max 1 card instead of 3. Reversible — a "Show more" link appears to toggle back.

P11. Confirmation After "Send Quote Request"

Decision: Toast notification using existing Sonner toast module: "Quote request sent to [vendor name]". Modal closes. Toast appears at top of screen for 4 seconds.

P12. Confirmation After "Add to Directory"

Decision: Button changes to green "In Directory" state (already in mockup). Plus toast notification: "[vendor name] added to your vendor directory". No link to vendor page — planner knows where their vendor page is.

ROOT ADMIN

A13. Campaign Detail View

Decision: Detail modal that opens when admin clicks a campaign row. Shows: full campaign settings, all creatives (with images), performance chart, lead list, vendor profile info. Click outside or X to close and return to campaign table. Button at top of modal: "Open in new tab" to see it as a full page.

A14. Vendor Profile View from Admin

Decision: Review card has a flip animation — front shows creative preview, back shows vendor business info (name, images, location, category, stats). A "View as vendor" button logs the admin into the vendor's portal so they see exactly what the vendor sees. This is also how the admin operates campaigns during the free tier.

A15. Admin Creates Campaign on Behalf of Vendor

Decision: Shortcut button on the root admin advertising dashboard: "Go to Vendor Portal" that links to the campaign portal. Admin can also use the "Log in as vendor" button from any vendor card. During free tier, the admin creates vendors and campaigns this way. No separate admin campaign creation form.

A16. Admin Notification for New Submissions

Decision: If admin is logged in: in-app bell notification with badge count on "Creative Review" sidebar link (already in mockup showing "5"). If admin is not logged in: email notification using existing email service. Both triggered when a vendor submits a campaign for review.

CROSS-PORTAL (Research-Based)

C17. click_id Generation and Persistence

Research: Google (GCLID) and Meta (fbclid) both generate unique IDs at the moment of click, store server-side and in cookies, carry through the full conversion funnel. Both use deterministic matching (click_id links click to conversion with certainty).

Decision: Generate UUIDv4 in the frontend SponsoredVendorCard component's onClick handler (not on render). Pass to every subsequent API call: ad_event (click), quote request modal (ad_lead), add-to-directory (ad_lead). Store on: ad_event.click_id, ad_lead.click_id, vendor_profile.attribution_click_id. Since everything happens within our platform (no cross-site), we don't need cookies or URL params — just pass the UUID through component props and API payloads.

C18. Impression Tracking

Research: IAB/MRC standard: 50% of pixels visible for 1+ continuous second = viewable impression. Both Google and Meta follow this. Standard web tool: IntersectionObserver API.

Decision: Fire impression_served on component mount (card rendered in DOM). Fire impression_viewable when IntersectionObserver reports ≥50% visibility for 1+ second. Only impression_viewable counts toward CTR. Both events include campaign_id and creative_id. Existing SponsoredVendorsRail already has IntersectionObserver — rewire to pass campaign_id.

C19. Campaign End/Expiration

Research: Google stops serving at end_date immediately via query filter, then changes status async. Meta does the same — immediate stop, async cleanup.

Decision: Two-layer approach. Layer 1 (real-time): the ad serving query filters end_date ≥ now, so expired campaigns stop showing instantly. Layer 2 (async): nightly cron scans for status: active AND end_date < now, sets status to "completed", sends vendor notification email with final campaign results.

C20. Frequency Capping

Research: 55% of users are discouraged from purchasing when bombarded with the same ad. CTR drops 30-50% after repeated exposure. Industry standard: 3 impressions per campaign per user per day for direct response.

Why it matters: Without capping, the same 3 vendors show to the same planner every page load, all day. Planner gets annoyed, hides everything, perception of your platform drops. Capping rotates vendors so planners see variety and all vendors get fair exposure.

Decision: Max 3 viewable impressions per campaign per planner per day. Enforced via Redis counter: key = freq:{planner_id}:{campaign_id}:{YYYY-MM-DD}, increment on viewable impression, check before serving. When cap hit, that campaign is excluded and next-ranked campaign fills the slot. Also: max 1 card per vendor per rail view (deduplicate by vendor_id).

EDGE CASES

E21. Vendor Edits Profile After Campaign Is Live

Research: Google requires re-review (3 business days) for creative changes on live campaigns. Meta restarts learning phase. Industry consensus: split profile from creative.

Decision: Two zones. Profile fields (business name, email, phone, website, location, category) update live across all campaigns — these are identity/contact fields. Creative fields (headline, description, offer, image, CTA) are frozen per campaign at submission. To change creative on a live campaign: vendor edits creative, it goes to review_status: pending, campaign keeps serving the previous approved creative until the new one is approved.

E22. Planner Already Has Vendor in Directory

Decision: Duplicate check before add. On page load, check planner's directory against displayed campaign vendor IDs. If vendor already exists in directory (regardless of source — organic or ad), show "Already in Directory" button (green, disabled) instead of "Add to Directory". Skip the add, no duplicate created.

E23. Multiple Campaigns from Same Vendor

Research: Google 2025 policy: one ad per advertiser per location. Universal best practice: deduplicate by advertiser in the same ad slot.

Decision: Deduplicate by vendor_id in ad serving. When get_servable_campaigns() returns results, group by vendor_id and pick only the highest-quality-score campaign per vendor. If Bloom & Petal has 3 active campaigns, only the best one shows in the rail. Others get rotated in on subsequent page loads once the first one hits its frequency cap for the day.
