Metrics Analysis — The Special Wedding

Root Admin Dashboard · Planner Dashboard · Couple Portal  |  April 2, 2026

Executive Summary

Your platform tracks a strong set of operational and infrastructure metrics across all three dashboards. The root admin SaaS page is the most mature, with user, subscription, and operations metrics at configurable timeframes. The planner dashboard delivers solid per-wedding health data with RSVP tracking and AI-powered insights. The couple portal gives end-users a comprehensive planning view.

The main gap across all three dashboards is the absence of behavioral product metrics — engagement frequency, retention cohorts, activation rates, and conversion funnels. The platform tracks what exists (counts of users, weddings, RSVPs) but not how people use it (login frequency, feature adoption, time-to-value). This limits your ability to diagnose churn, prioritize features, or measure onboarding improvements.

Recommended North Star Metric

Weekly Active Weddings with Guest Engagement

Count of weddings where at least one guest RSVP, form submission, or media upload occurred in the past 7 days. This captures value delivery to both planners (active weddings = revenue) and couples/guests (engagement = product utility).

This metric is value-aligned (moves when your product delivers planning outcomes), leading (active guest engagement predicts subscription retention), and actionable (the team can improve onboarding, reminders, and RSVP flows to drive it). Currently you track active_weddings count but not the engagement dimension — weddings can be "active" without any guest interaction happening.

Assessment

The root admin dashboard has two strong layers: (1) a solid infrastructure monitoring suite with real-time health, error tracking, and endpoint performance, and (2) a decent SaaS analytics view with user/subscription/operations counts at configurable timeframes (7d/30d/90d/1y). The historical snapshot system provides trend data over time.

The critical gap is the entire behavioral layer. You have "total users: 150" and "active users: 120" but no visibility into how those users engage, when they drop off, or what predicts retention. Without cohort analysis and funnel metrics, you can't answer questions like "are newer planners retaining better than earlier ones?" or "what's our trial-to-paid conversion rate this month?"

Top 3 Additions for Root Admin
  • Activation funnel: Signup → Profile setup → First wedding created → First guest invited → First RSVP received. Track conversion at each step.
  • Retention cohorts: Weekly signup cohorts with D7/D30/D60/D90 retention. Show as a cohort table or curve chart.
  • Revenue breakdown: Add ARPU, net revenue retention (NRR), and MRR movement chart (new + expansion − contraction − churn).

Assessment

The planner dashboard is well-designed as a wedding operations command center. The four summary cards, per-wedding progress tracking, and AI-powered insights give planners a clear view of their portfolio health. The progress percentage calculation (40% RSVP + 20% guest list + 20% events + 20% details) is a thoughtful composite metric.

What's missing is couple engagement visibility. A planner can see that 60 of 150 RSVPs are pending, but can't see whether the couple has logged into the portal this week, whether they've completed the shared forms, or how quickly they respond to messages. These signals help planners triage which couples need attention. Similarly, there's no aggregate view of "how healthy is my practice?" beyond revenue — metrics like average RSVP response rate across weddings or on-time task completion would help planners benchmark their performance.

Top 3 Additions for Planner Dashboard
  • Couple engagement signals: Last login date, form completion status, and average message response time per wedding — surfaced as "needs attention" flags.
  • RSVP velocity: Show RSVPs received per day as a trend line per wedding. A flattening curve signals the planner needs to send reminders.
  • Practice health score: Composite across weddings — on-time milestones, budget adherence, guest response rates. One number to show overall practice performance.

Assessment

The couple portal is feature-rich and well-structured with 8 sections covering the full wedding planning lifecycle. The quick stats grid (6 cards) gives an immediate snapshot, and the guest tracker with per-event RSVP breakdown is genuinely useful. Budget tracking with color-coded thresholds (red >90%, amber >70%) provides clear visual signals.

The main gap is the absence of a progress/readiness score. Couples want to know "am I on track?" The portal shows individual data points (RSVPs, budget, forms) but doesn't synthesize them into an overall readiness indicator. The planner dashboard has a progress percentage per wedding, but this isn't exposed to the couple. Additionally, couples have no visibility into guest-side engagement — they can't see which guests opened their emails or viewed the RSVP page, which would help them know who to follow up with personally.

Top 3 Additions for Couple Portal
  • Wedding readiness score: Surface the progress % already calculated on the planner side. Show it prominently with a breakdown of what's driving the score.
  • Budget category chart: A simple donut or bar chart showing spend by category (you already collect budget_categories). Couples want to see where money is going at a glance.
  • "Who hasn't responded" list: A filterable view of guests who haven't RSVP'd, sorted by days since invite sent. Makes personal follow-up easy.

Dashboard Design Observations

What's Working Well

  • Configurable time ranges on SaaS analytics (7d/30d/90d/1y)
  • Role-based access control — planners only see their weddings
  • Color-coded budget thresholds on couple portal
  • AI insights provide actionable, prioritized recommendations
  • Per-event RSVP breakdown in couple portal
  • Real-time activity feeds on both admin and planner dashboards
  • Infrastructure monitoring with p50/p95/p99 latency breakdowns

Anti-Patterns to Address

  • No comparison context: User counts shown as raw numbers without previous-period comparison or targets
  • Flat hierarchy: All metrics presented at the same visual weight — no clear North Star at the top
  • Output over outcome: "Notifications sent" and "AI events" are output metrics, not user outcome metrics
  • Missing trend lines: Most metrics are point-in-time snapshots without week-over-week directional indicators
  • No alerting: No threshold-based alerts for metrics (e.g., response rate dropping below 50%)

Bottom Line

Your dashboards are operationally solid — the infrastructure monitoring, RSVP tracking, and wedding operations data are genuinely useful and well-presented. The foundation is strong. The primary gap is the product analytics layer: engagement, activation, retention, and conversion metrics that would tell you whether the product is growing and why. The good news is that most of the raw data (user actions, timestamps, wedding lifecycle events) likely already exists in your database — it just needs to be aggregated into the right views.

Start with items 1–3 in the priority table (low effort, high impact), then invest in WAU instrumentation (item 4) as the foundation for everything else.
